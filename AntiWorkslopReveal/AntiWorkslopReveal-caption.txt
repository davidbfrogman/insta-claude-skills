MAIN CAPTION
====================================================================

The most expensive thing AI can do for your career is make you look productive.

Researchers at BetterUp and Stanford gave the problem a name: workslop. AI-generated work that looks finished but quietly pushes the actual thinking onto whoever opens it.

It costs the person receiving it about two hours to untangle. That part is recoverable.

This part isn’t. Roughly half the people who get workslop rate the sender as less capable and less reliable than they did before. A third decide they’d rather not work with them again. And almost nobody says anything, so you never find out it happened.

Three ways to stay on the right side of that:

01. Would they know what they’re looking at? If the person opening it has to reconstruct your thinking, you didn’t finish the work. You moved it onto them.

02. Generic in, generic out. If anyone at any company could have typed your prompt, it shows in everything that comes back.

03. Strategize with it. Don’t outsource to it. AI is excellent right up to the decision. The decision is the part you’re paid for.

None of this is an argument against using AI. The heaviest users get the most out of it. The difference is that they use it to do the work instead of to avoid it.
